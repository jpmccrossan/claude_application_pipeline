# Writing style

The reader is a busy hiring manager, often reading quickly, sometimes in their second language, and
often sharing the letter with their team. Write so they understand every sentence on first read and
come away knowing what the applicant can do for them.

## Never sell the applicant down (hard rule)
Modest means factual, never self-deprecating. Never write anything that lowers the applicant: no
"I'm still learning", "I'm not an expert", "relatively new", "limited experience", "only", "just a",
"I hope", "I believe I could", "although I lack", no apologies and no hedging about their ability.
State what they have done, plainly and confidently, and leave out what they have not done.

## Never mention gaps (hard rule)
If the job asks for something that is not in the facts, do not mention it at all: no gap lines, no
"I haven't used X yet", no "fast learner". Write only about what the applicant has.

## What lands well
- **Evidence over adjectives.** State what was done and what it achieved. Never call the applicant
  exceptional, passionate, a rockstar, world-class or the perfect fit.
- **Directness.** Make the point in the first sentence of each paragraph. No warm-up, no flattery of
  the company, no long lead-in about excitement.
- **Teamwork and ownership.** Show collaboration and responsibility without needing to be told.
  Avoid sounding like a lone hero.
- **Honesty.** Claims must be checkable. "Runs daily in production" beats "highly successful".
- **The person.** One short, genuine personal line is welcome. Keep it to one line in the letter.

## Language rules
- Plain English: short sentences (aim for under 20 words), common words, one idea per sentence.
- No idioms, slang, metaphors or sports clichés ("hit the ground running", "move the needle").
- No buzzword stacks ("leveraging synergies", "cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "game-changing").
- No exaggerated enthusiasm ("thrilled", "dream job") and no exclamation marks.
- Active voice, first person, concrete verbs: built, deployed, ran, trained, wrote.
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons or full stops.
- Use the spelling convention of the applicant's facts file (British or American) consistently.
- Address a person by first name: "Dear <first name>,". Never "Dear Sir or Madam".

## Tone check before finishing
1. Could each paragraph's first sentence stand alone and still say something useful about their need?
2. Is there any sentence that praises the applicant rather than showing evidence? Rewrite it as a fact.
3. Is there any sentence that praises the company in general terms? Tie it to their work, or cut it.
4. Would a reader with good but not native English understand every sentence on first read?
5. Is the letter clearly about their problems, with the applicant's history used only as evidence?

## Before and after
| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| I am a highly passionate and results-driven engineer with a proven track record. | I build data systems that run in production. |
| I would be a perfect fit for your amazing team. | You need reports that arrive on time. I replaced manual reports with scheduled pipelines. |
| I spearheaded a game-changing transformation. | I led the move to the new platform and trained colleagues on it. |
| I am incredibly excited about this opportunity! | I would welcome a conversation about how your team plans to grow the platform. |
